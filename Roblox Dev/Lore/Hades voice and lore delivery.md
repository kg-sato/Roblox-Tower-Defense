# Hades: character voice and lore delivery (T031)

Question: how does Hades deliver character voice and lore in short pieces, through relationships, and across unlock pacing, and what should our familiars and NPCs take?

## Facts (sourced)
- No narrator; the story is delivered through voiced dialogue between Zagreus and Underworld and Olympian characters. About ten hours of branching dialogue were drafted; conversations change with past events, with payoffs back at the House of Hades, and dialogue advances with each run. [Wikipedia, Hades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game)) (fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 118,331 characters; the rest is categories and awards).
- Hades has a large fully voiced cast; characters react to the player's current situation and to other gods met in a run, so relationships emerge from randomised encounters. Writing is scripted in pieces but the combinations still surprise Kasavin. Lines are unlikely to repeat until a player has looped the game roughly 20 to 30 times. Some story moments happen once, others repeat. Early-access feedback showed which characters resonated and whether the humour landed. Kasavin (Supergiant creative director) contrasts this with Pyre, which got feedback only after launch. [GeekDad, interview with Greg Kasavin](https://geekdad.com/2019/10/narrative-and-early-access-supergiants-greg-kasavin-discusses-hades-development/) (31 Oct 2019; fetched 2026-10-28).
- From search snippets (unread, 403 or not fetched): Kasavin trimmed the story to fit the loop and put effort into a clear personality and interests for each god, shade and gorgon, expressed as comments on the player's progress ([ScreenHub interview](https://www.screenhub.com.au/news-article/features/digital/jini-maxwell/hades-greg-kasavin-breaks-down-supergiants-unique-approach-to-narrative-262459), 403); he worked closely with audio director Darren Korb from early on, and a GDC 2021 talk "Breathing Life into Greek Myth" covers how distinct voices were found ([GDC Vault](https://gdcvault.com/play/1026975/Breathing-Life-into-Greek-Myth), listing only).
- The game won the Hugo Award for Best Game or Interactive Work and the Nebula for Best Game Writing (category tags on Wikipedia).

## Not found
- Line length numbers, the unlock order of story beats, and voice direction details; none were stated in the pages read.

## Takeaways for us (recommendations, not sourced)
1. Short lines per familiar (T098): three trigger lines (summon, attack, victory), plus reactions keyed to events the player already did: first merge, a boss kill, a faction pairing (T068).
2. Relationships by data: a table of pair lines keyed on who is in the roster or on the same stage (T068), so combinations multiply without writing every case.
3. Repeat-avoidance: track lines shown per profile and pick unseen ones first; lines should not repeat for many plays (Hades' 20 to 30 loops); store the seen set in the profile with a size cap (server decides, T186 pattern).
4. Unlock pacing: give a one-time story beat at set progress points (stage clears) and repeatable banter between them; hold the main reveal for the late game, in line with the cosmic turn (T054, T066).
5. Playtest which characters people mention first and double down, as early access did.
