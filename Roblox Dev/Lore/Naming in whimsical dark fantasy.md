# Naming in whimsical dark fantasy games (T029)

Question: how do dark fantasy games name creatures, places and bosses so the names are memorable and not generic, and what naming rules should we use?

The name lists below are read from Wikipedia pages fetched 2026-10-28. The patterns drawn from them are my reading, not statements from the games' authors; no naming interview was found.

## Facts (sourced): sample names
**Darkest Dungeon** (released 19 Jan 2016, Red Hook Studios). Classes: Arbalest, Man at Arms, Musketeer, Shieldbreaker. Places: The Hamlet, the mansion, the catacombs beneath it, The Darkest Dungeon, the Butcher's Circus. Bosses: The Fanatic, The Heart of Darkness, an "Avatar of the Crawling Chaos". A sardonic narrator (Wayne June) comments throughout; tone is gothic Lovecraftian horror centred on psychological stress. [Wikipedia, Darkest Dungeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darkest_Dungeon)

**Cult of the Lamb** (released 11 Aug 2022). Bishops: Leshy, Heket, Kallamar, Shamura, plus Narinder, who is The One Who Waits. Others: Ratau, Baal and Aym, and in the Woolhaven expansion Ewefall, the town of Woolhaven, Yngya and Marchosias. Dark themes with cute visuals (IGN praised the balance of macabre and "cutesy cartoon vibes"). [Wikipedia, Cult of the Lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_of_the_Lamb)

**Bloodborne** (released 24 Mar 2015 in North America). Places: Yharnam, the Hunter's Dream, Byrgenwerth, Yahar'gul, the Healing Church. Figures: Rom the One Reborn, Micolash, Mergo's Wet Nurse, Gehrman, Lady Maria, Queen Yharnam, the Moon Presence, Kos. The page says names blend Victorian Gothic terms such as "Hunter" and "blood ministration" with Lovecraftian ideas such as the Great Ones and the Old Blood, tied to the game's inspirations including Dracula and Lovecraft. [Wikipedia, Bloodborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodborne) (first 100,000 of 109,821 characters read)

## Patterns I see in these lists (inference)
1. **Plain-word titles for roles:** The Fanatic, The Heart of Darkness, the Hunter, the Butcher's Circus. A common noun plus "the/The" is easy to say and remember.
2. **Invented proper names for unique beings:** Yharnam, Micolash, Narinder, Kallamar, Yahar'gul. Two or three syllables, one unusual sound.
3. **Epithets as a second name:** Rom, the One Reborn; the One Who Waits; Mergo's Wet Nurse. A job or a relationship tells the story in four words.
4. **Cute base plus dark twist** (Cult of the Lamb): an animal or a child's word with a heavy role (Lamb, Woolhaven, Ewefall) gives the whimsical-dark mix.
5. **Real-world flavour words** (Victorian, church, hunter) ground fantasy names.

## Naming rules for our game (recommendations, not sourced)
1. Familiars: a short given name (1 to 2 syllables, ours: Ember, Ward, Gargoyle are plain; avoid more like these) plus an epithet in the card line, for example "the Lamp that Hesitates". Keep the name distinct from every other card in first letter and sound.
2. Places: Lampwick Lane style, an object plus a street word; keep the five stage sets in that pattern (Harbour of Brass, Silent Cathedral, Clockwork Foundry).
3. Bosses: a title with "the" plus a job (the Clockmaker, the Regent); save the proper name for the reveal.
4. Check each new name against a "would a stranger remember this after one hearing" test, and against existing game names so we do not echo a famous one. Names above are shown for pattern only; do not reuse any.
5. Write all names, epithets and place words in the glossary (T058) and tone guide (T057).
