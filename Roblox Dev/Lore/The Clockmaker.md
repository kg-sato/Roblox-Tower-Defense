# The Clockmaker: main antagonist, motive and five-act reveal (T059)

Status: draft written overnight 2026-10-28. The brief (a clockmaker who stopped time over the city, a motive and a five-act reveal plan) comes from the task list. Everything else, including the name and the twist, is a proposal for the user to accept, change or cut. It extends [[World Bible]], [[Factions]], [[Stage Set Lore]] and [[The Cosmic Turn]]. Tone follows [[Tone Guide]]: loss implied, no gore.

## Who they are
- **Title:** the Clockmaker. A proper name is withheld until act 5: **Ysolde Marrow** (proposal; short, concrete, not taken from another game).
- **Role:** founder of the Clockwork Guild and builder of the Great Clock. The city's hours were honest because of them.
- **Look:** a tall figure in a long coat of pocket-watch chains, spectacles with a cracked lens, hands always in motion winding something that is not there. A stopped watch at the chest. Palette: brass and ash, with a thin line of white at the edges as the cosmic turn nears.
- **Gesture and flaw (per [[Character Bible Rules]]):** winds one more turn before every action. Flaw: cannot let a moment end, including their own mistakes.
- **Voice:** gentle, exact, apologetic. Never shouts. Sample line: "One more turn, and it will hold."

## Motive
On the night of the banked furnaces, the Clockmaker lost someone they were responsible for (the apprentice who kept the Foundry's lamps; left unspecified beyond that). They stopped the Great Clock one moment before it happened, intending a pause of an hour. The pause did not end, and the stopped moment began to leak as the fog. The Clockmaker now keeps the clock stopped because restarting it means the loss happens. They are not cruel; they are holding a door shut with their whole body.
- **What they want:** the clock never to move; if it must, to run the stopped hour again and change it.
- **What they fear:** that the pause was the mistake, and that the apprentice would not have wanted it.
- **Why they are an enemy:** the fog and the stopped city cost everyone else their hours, and the Clockmaker will undo merges and roll back the player's progress to keep the moment still.

## Five-act reveal plan
Each act matches a stage set and its boss (T143 to T147). One new clue per act; no clue is delivered by a text wall (see [[Dishonored and Prey environmental lore]]).
| Act | Stages / set | Boss | What the player learns | Clue form |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1 to 5, Lampwick Lane | The Unbound | The clock stopped on purpose, not by accident | Street clock with hands removed, not broken |
| 2 | 6 to 10, Harbour of Brass | the Clockmaker's Automaton | Someone built machines to keep working through the stopped hour | The automaton winds itself by hand-cranks that are fresh and clean |
| 3 | 11 to 15, Silent Cathedral | the Star Choir's Echo | The Choir knows what was lost and wants the city to sing it out | A covered statue with a child's lantern at its feet |
| 4 | 16 to 20, Clockwork Foundry | the Hollow Court Regent | The furnaces were banked, not put out; the loss happened here | The cold furnace with one lit door and a small coat on a hook |
| 5 | 21 to 25, Above the Grey Veil | the Clockmaker | Their name, their motive, and the choice | The clock face seen from behind; the apprentice's lamp inside it |

Reveal order: who built the clock (act 1 caption), that someone was lost (act 4), that the Clockmaker stopped it themselves (act 5). The player is told in act 1 that the clock "stopped"; the word "stopped it" is withheld until act 5.

## Boss mechanics tie-in
- The final fight reverses one merge per wave (T147). The lantern flare from [[The Cosmic Turn]] lets the player hold a merge in place; both mechanics echo the theme of holding or losing a moment.
- Phase beats: act 1 denial (rolls back), act 2 bargaining (offers a pause), act 3 anger (fog thickens), act 4 sorrow (the fog thins), act 5 acceptance (the clock moves). This is a loose map to grief stages, not a claim about the games.

## Ending options (for T072)
1. **Restart:** the lantern lets the clock tick; the Foundry's lost hour passes and the stars come out.
2. **Hold:** the Clockmaker is persuaded to keep the pause for one more night; the sky stays open but the clock stays stopped.
3. **Closing scene only (the plan's default):** no player choice; the Apprentice's lamp is lit and the final caption is `[ THE CLOCK TURNS ]` or `[ ONE MORE TURN ]`.
The user chooses; I recommend option 3 for v1.
