# World Bible: Lampwick Lane, the fog, the clock, the familiars (T052)

Status: draft written overnight 2026-10-28. It builds on the user's decided theme arc in [[Game Design Doc]] (steampunk and quiet magic at the start, openly cosmic at the end; the loading-screen story of a clock tunnel, a future tree and watching eyes; a brass lantern as the protected item) and on the stage list in [[Backgrounds and Enemies - Mystic Victorian]]. Everything below that is not in those notes is a proposal for the user to accept or change.

## The city
Lampwick Lane is the oldest street of a gaslit port city that has no name on any map, because the maps were written before the fog. Brass pipes run under the cobbles carrying lamp-gas and something else that hums. The street is lit by hand: every lamp has a keeper, and every keeper answers to the Lantern Keepers. Five districts descend from the Lane: the Harbour of Brass, the Silent Cathedral, the Clockwork Foundry and, above it all, the Grey Veil.

## The fog
The fog arrived the night the great clock stopped. It is not weather. It is the city's lost time, spilled: wherever it lies thick, people forget the hour, then the day, then their names. Lamplight pushes it back a few paces. The fog is what the player defends against; it hides lanes, softens edges and, near the Grey Veil, shows faces that are not there. In the late game the fog thins and the sky behind it is not sky.

## The clock
At the centre of the city stands the Great Clock, built by the Clockmaker to keep the city's hours honest. It stopped at one moment and has never moved since. The hands point at no hour that exists. The clock is the reason time feels wrong everywhere: shops open and close on their own, the dusk on Lampwick Lane never finishes. Whoever restarts it decides what time the city lives in. The loading tunnel of gears is the clock seen from inside.

## Why the familiars exist
Time leaves a residue where it is cut: small, patient beings that were never born and never die, called familiars. Each is made of the thing that kept its hour: a flame, a stone watcher, a child of frost, a cog, a mirror, a moth of light. They answer only to a lit lantern. A Lantern Keeper does not command them; the lantern gives them a place to stand while the fog is near. Merging two familiars is how the lantern teaches them a longer hour, which is why they grow. The cosmic familiars (the Star Drake and its kin) are what the city's time looks like from outside it.

## What the player is
A new Lantern Keeper, the youngest, handed a brass lantern with an empty glass. The lantern is the thing to protect; if it is put out, the fog wins the street. The player's skill tree is the future that the lantern shows when it burns bright, which is the tree the loading tunnel promises.

## Tone and limits
Whimsical and dark in the Cult of the Lamb sense: soft shapes and warm light, with the danger carried by objects and text, never gore. Names stay short and concrete, per [[Naming in whimsical dark fantasy]]. Open questions for the user: who the Clockmaker is (T059 proposes the antagonist), whether the fog ends at stage 25, and whether the eyes in the loading tunnel are the Star Choir (T053 and T054).
