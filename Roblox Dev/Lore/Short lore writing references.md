# How strong games write short lore: item text, bios, environment (T028)

Question: what do strong games do with short lore text, and what rules should our familiar cards, item text and stage lore follow?

**Gap against the task:** only two Wikipedia pages were read (a definition and a one-line statement about Dark Souls). The craft advice sources (PC Gamer, a 2013 flavor essay, a GDC narrative paper) were unreadable (truncated, 404, binary PDF, connection refused). The craft rules below come from search snippets and my own reasoning, and are labelled.

## Facts (sourced)
- Environmental storytelling means story conveyed through the physical design or set dressing of a game's world rather than cutscenes or dialogue. [Wikipedia, Glossary of video game terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_storytelling) (fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 225,175 characters read; the origin and technique sections were not reached).
- Dark Souls: the plot is conveyed mainly through environmental details, item flavor text and dialogue with non-player characters; players piece together clues rather than receive cutscenes. Series creator Hidetaka Miyazaki is credited as director of the first game and Dark Souls III. The page names no other writers. [Wikipedia, Dark Souls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Souls) (fetched 2026-10-28).

## Search-snippet claims (unverified, not read in full)
- Item flavor text lets a writer introduce many characters without overwhelming the audience ([Hipsters of the Coast, "Flavor makes the world"](https://www.hipstersofthecoast.com/2022/07/flavor-makes-the-world/), July 2022 per URL).
- Good flavor text is concise, one or two lines at most ([Flare RPG, "Using item flavor to tell stories"](https://flarerpg.org/2013/02/07/20130207/), 7 Feb 2013 per URL; page now returns 404).
- A GDC narrative review credits Dark Souls' subtle, indirect storytelling and says exposition by dialogue is rare ([GDC Vault, narrative review PDF, 2017](https://media.gdcvault.com/gdc2017/GameNarrativeReview/Calvin_Walantus_UCSC_GDC_Narrative.pdf); the PDF text could not be read).
- Miyazaki is reported to want players to build their own narrative from game elements ([Prima Games, Five keys to Dark Souls lore](https://primagames.com/featured/five-keys-understanding-dark-souls-lore), snippet only).
- PC Gamer has a piece on the craft of flavour text ([The art of flavour text](https://www.pcgamer.com/the-art-of-flavour-text/)), body unreadable.

## Rules for our lore text (recommendations, not sourced)
1. Familiar card line: one sentence, 15 to 25 words; the long bio is 120 to 200 words in the collection (T097), matching the task list.
2. State a fact, imply the feeling: name a place, an object and one odd detail; let the player infer the loss or danger.
3. Each item or stage text should point to one other thing in the world (a faction, a place, a name) so lines link up like Dark Souls' scattered clues (glossary T058).
4. Put story in props: broken lamps, covered statues, clock hands stopped at one time (T055, T071) so stage art carries lore without text.
5. Ban list and tone examples live in the tone guide (T057); do not copy phrasing from any game.
6. Before locking this, read the Dark Souls item-description writing in the game itself or in the GDC paper, which I could not read.
