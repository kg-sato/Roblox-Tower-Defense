# Dishonored and Prey: environmental lore, objects that tell stories (T035)

Question: how do Dishonored and Prey carry lore in objects and places, and what should our stage sets and props do?

## Facts (sourced)
**Dishonored** (released 9 Oct 2012 in North America). Dunwall is a fictional plague-ridden industrial city, drawn from late-19th and early-20th-century London and Edinburgh; whale oil powers the city and its weapons; the plague is spread by rats; Tallboy guards and Walls of Light keep order. The setting began as medieval Japan, then 1666 London, and became a fictional city once 20th-century technology was added. Visual design director Viktor Antonov and art director Sébastien Mitton photographed real side streets and alleys and designed "from a rat's viewpoint"; painters Grimshaw, Canaletto and Doré influenced the mood. Whaling ships were added partly to show whales in the world. Lore delivery on the page: the Heart narrates secrets and history of locations and characters; ambient dialogue adds background; loudspeakers broadcast propaganda; the world reacts to player choices, with more rats and plague victims and new scenes. The first 100,000 of 233,733 characters were read and mention no collectible notes or books. [Wikipedia, Dishonored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dishonored) (fetched 2026-10-28).

**Prey** (launched 5 May 2017). Talos I is a space station that began as a Soviet-American prison for aliens and was refit by TranStar as a research lab; it is a continuous space modelled on a "mega-dungeon" idea from Arkane's Arx Fatalis. The look is Art Deco: artist Sébastien Mitton called it "Neo Deco", mixing 1960s American retrofuturism, Soviet-era brutalism and lavish executive spaces. Much backstory comes from optional environmental sources (books, notes, emails, terminals, audio logs); players who skip them still get the core plot. NPC backstories, including characters already dead at the start, are told through objects and records; cutscenes were kept minimal. [Wikipedia, Prey (2017)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prey_(2017_video_game)) (fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 147,506 characters).

**Snippets (not read in full):** Antonov's philosophy per PC Gamer: "everything is a character" ([PC Gamer preview](https://www.pcgamer.com/au/dishonored-preview-power-possession-and-pacifism-in-a-hand-crafted-world)); art direction pieces at [Game Informer, "The look and feel of Dishonored"](https://gameinformer.com/b/features/archive/2011/07/20/the-look-and-feel-of-dishonored) (20 Jul 2011) and [GamesBeat](https://gamesbeat.com/how-dishonoreds-artists-created-an-oppressive-world-from-scratch/) (unread).

## Recommendations for us (not sourced rules)
1. Put the story in the board art: each stage set gets three or four props that imply an event (a stopped clock, a candle row burned to one side, a harbour crane holding a coffin) and no text, matching T055 and T162.
2. Make the core plot readable without the optional lore (Prey's rule): the captions carry the main beat; collectible lore fragments (T071) add depth.
3. Let the world change with progress (Dishonored's rats and plague scale with player choices): fog thickens or lamps relight between stages, tied to the cosmic turn (T054).
4. Use a single narrated voice sparingly for place history (the Heart pattern): the Lantern could whisper one line when a stage loads.
5. Start from one clear visual mix per set and write it as a sentence in the stage bible, as Prey does with "Neo Deco" (T244).
