# Hollow Knight: creature design (T004)

Question: how does Hollow Knight build readable creatures, tell lore through visuals and hold a limited look, and what should our familiars take from it?

## Facts (sourced)
- Released on Windows 24 Feb 2017. Artist Ari Gibson scanned hand-drawn sketches straight into the engine; the team kept the design simple to protect the schedule. Most NPCs are bug-themed; enemies and bosses are numerous and varied. [Wikipedia, Hollow Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollow_Knight) (fetched 2026-10-28).
- Story comes from NPC talk, environmental imagery and scattered writings; signage is minimal. Same source.
- The 2018 Lifeblood update changed the colour palette; the original palette is not described. Same source.
- The 150-plus enemies are described as "tiny bugs", each with a distinct look and attack style. The Knight is kept graphic and basic because it is drawn constantly. A small cloak hides the arms, so they need no drawing or animation (a shape-driven simplification; the article does not say "silhouette"). [MCV/DEVELOP, "When We Made... Hollow Knight"](https://mcvuk.com/when-we-made-hollow-knight/) (31 July 2018; fetched 2026-10-28).
- The team wanted any broken wall or corner to feel like it could hide something, and cites Dark Souls for oppressive mood. Same source; it does not explain how the mood is made in art.
- The Knight began in the game jam entry Hungry Knight. Search summary of [Game Informer, "The Making of Hollow Knight"](https://gameinformer.com/2018/10/16/the-making-of-hollow-knight) (16 Oct 2018; not read in full).

## Not found
- No read source describes the colour palette rules or an explicit silhouette rule. "Limited palette" in the task title is unsupported by these sources.

## Takeaways for us (recommendations, not facts)
1. Simplify the body to the fewest shapes the animation budget allows; hide hard parts (cloak, shell, glass) as Hollow Knight does with the arms. Fits our under-20,000-triangle limit.
2. Give each creature one distinct look and one attack style (T074, T133).
3. Put lore on props and the world (marks, broken objects, notes) instead of text boxes (T052, T055, T071).
4. Do not claim a palette rule from this game; set our own (one hue per character, T056).
