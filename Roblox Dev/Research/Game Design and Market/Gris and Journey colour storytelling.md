# Gris and Journey: colour storytelling (T034)

Question: do Gris and Journey assign one hue per emotion, and how can our five stage sets use colour to tell story?

**Finding:** the Wikipedia pages do not state a one-hue-per-emotion rule for either game. The task's premise is unconfirmed here. Journey's art director is quoted on colour guiding emotion, and Gris ties the return of colour to progress.

## Facts (sourced)
- Gris (released 13 Dec 2018 on macOS, Switch and Windows; PS5 and Xbox 13 Dec 2022): the narrative deals with grief and the five stages, and the team consulted a psychologist. Colour returns to the world as Gris reaches four areas and restores statues there. Her abilities come from collected star-like lights (stone form, constellation paths), not from colours. The page does not say red, green, blue and yellow represent specific stages of grief. Director and artist Conrad Roset co-founded Nomada Studio; to make the early colourless areas appealing the team drew on Iwagumi-style aquariums; they dropped a plan to change her dress as colours unlocked, partly because of camera angles. [Wikipedia, Gris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gris_(video_game)) (fetched 2026-10-28).
- Journey (PS3 13 Mar 2012; Windows 6 Jun 2019): art director Matt Nava with creative director Jenova Chen chose a cultural middle ground drawing on Arabic architecture with Chinese and Tibetan influences. The scarf's runes show its charge and turn from dull grey to vibrant red when the player sings near it. The story follows Joseph Campbell's hero's journey as stages of life; 3 of 25 testers cried at the end. The page says little about colour by stage. [Wikipedia, Journey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_(2012_video_game)) (fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 116,741 characters).
- From search snippets (not read in full): Nava is quoted as saying colour was used to guide the players' emotions and that the palette varies across the journey; a Spanish article on the 10th anniversary says the sky began green and a sky blue was held back for the end ([Computer Graphics World via search](https://digital.copcomm.com/i/77744/44), [Vida Extra](https://www.vidaextra.com/aventura-plataformas/se-cumplen-10-anos-inolvidable-viaje-journey-su-director-artistico-comparte-artes-conceptuales-sus-paisajes)); Nava's GDC 2013 art talk is listed on [GDC Vault](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1017799/contactUs).

## Not found
- A per-stage palette list for Journey, or any Nomada Studio colour interview. Colour-to-emotion assignments are not documented in the pages I could read.

## What to take (recommendations, not sourced rules)
1. Start stages nearly colourless and add colour as the player progresses, as Gris does: our five sets could each introduce one new hue as they are cleared, ending in the cosmic turn (T054, T154).
2. Use one signature colour per set as a mood anchor and hold the strongest colour back for the end (Journey's saved sky blue), then check it with the palette work in [[Colour theory for readable teams]].
3. Let one prop carry the colour change (Journey's scarf; our lantern) so the player sees progress without text.
4. Plan the colour arc as a table (set, mood, key hue, light level) in the stage bible (T244) so assignments are deliberate, since the sources give no ready table to borrow.
5. Check every colour cue against colour-blind safety; hue alone is not enough (see the accessibility note).
