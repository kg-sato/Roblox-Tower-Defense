# Plants vs Zombies: character readability (T003)

Question: how does PvZ make each plant readable at small size with one idea per plant, and what should our familiars take from it?

## Facts (sourced)
- Released 5 May 2009 (PC, Mac) by PopCap; George Fan is the creator. [Wikipedia, Plants vs. Zombies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plants_vs._Zombies) (fetched 2026-10-28). The page has no design notes on readability.
- Plants have distinct jobs: peashooter basic damage, snow pea slows, cherry bomb area burst, wall-nut blocks, squash and lily pad counter the snorkeling zombie. [GBGames, "Plants vs Zombies: How Did PopCap Do it?"](https://www.gbgames.com/2009/05/22/plants-vs-zombies-how-did-popcap-do-it/) (22 May 2009; fetched 2026-10-28). The post infers this from play; it does not state a design rule.
- Same post: plants animate on action and zombies visibly break apart when destroyed (damage feedback); each character has a recognisable look that carries the humour. It also says some later plants feel pointless, so one-idea-per-plant failed in places.
- Fan gave GDC 2012 talks on appealing to new and hardcore players and on tutorial tips. [GDC news, 2012](https://gdconf.com/news/gdc_2012_reveals_plants_vs_zom) (search summary only; content not read).

## Not found
- No source read states a silhouette-at-small-size rule or an explicit "one idea per plant" rule. The PC Gamer making-of ([link](https://www.pcgamer.com/making-of-plants-vs-zombies)) returned only its title to the fetch tool, so Fan's own words are unread.

## Takeaways for us (recommendations, not facts)
1. Name each familiar's job in one verb before drawing it; if two share a verb, merge or change one (ties to T074, T087).
2. Make the job visible in the shape (a shooter has a muzzle-like feature, a blocker is wide and round); test in greyscale at 64 px (T087).
3. Show state on the body: action animation and a visible break or fade on defeat give readability without text.
4. Watch for dead roster slots: the GBGames critique suggests a late roster needs a role each, not just a new look.
