## Star charts: constellations as creatures
Source: [Wikipedia, Celestial cartography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_cartography) (fetched 2026-10-28).
- Constellations were drawn as mythic figures from c. 1 CE (Hyginus); Bayer's Uranometria (1603) kept figures but placed them on measured star positions; Schiller (1627) re-themed the same sky with Christian figures; Flamsteed's Atlas Coelestis appeared in 1729.
- Use: a constellation drawn as a creature is a ready design pattern for the Star Drake and mirror-cat (T078, T081); a re-skin of the same sky is a model for the cosmic turn recolouring the city (T054).
- Limit: the page gives no colours, borders or typefaces.

## Orrery: cosmic clockwork
Source: [Wikipedia, Orrery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orrery) (fetched 2026-10-28).
- Brass arms from a central spindle, a globe sun in the centre, gear trains, armillary rings, tilted-axis Earth and Moon. The name comes from a device c. 1712 for the Earl of Orrery. Benjamin Martin's 1764 design used brass arms on concentric tubes. Wright's candlelit lecture painting (c. 1766) shows a glowing central sun.
- Use: bridges the Victorian brass look and the cosmic endgame, so the sky opening can look like the clock showing its orrery. Concentric orbit rings fit skill-tree and boss UI (T059, T100).

## Zodiac: a ready icon set
Source: [Wikipedia, Zodiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zodiac) (fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 174,158 characters read).
- Twelve signs: ram, bull, twins, crab, lion, maiden, scales, scorpion, archer, goat or sea-goat, water-bearer, fish. Introduced by Babylonian astronomers around 401 BC; the Dendera zodiac (c. 50 BC) is the first known classical depiction. Most signs are animals or hybrids.
- Twelve signs match our twelve familiars (T074): pairing each familiar with a sign would give the cast an icon, a motif and a lore hook. This is an idea, not something the source says. Check for overlap with the "no fox, no sprout" rule.
- The page does not cover element groupings (fire, earth, air, water).
