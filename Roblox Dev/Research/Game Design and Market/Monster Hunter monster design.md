# Monster Hunter monster design (T033)

Question: how does Monster Hunter give monsters animal logic and readable attacks and weak points, and what should our enemies and bosses take?

**Scope note:** the task names Monster Hunter Rise, but the design statements found come from World-era interviews. The Rise Wikipedia page lists monsters and the Wirebug and has nothing on design or weak points. The Monster Hunter series is the shared source of the design approach.

## Facts (sourced)
- World's process: each new monster starts from a gameplay challenge the player must learn to exploit; level designers build an area that shows the behaviour, which shapes the monster's look. Monsters follow rules for interacting with their environment rather than scripted events; their tactics change as they weaken (more aggressive, or flee to rest). Research level raises as you investigate a monster and reveals its strengths, weaknesses and behaviour. Director Yuya Tokuda wanted players to observe behaviour and learn to exploit it, with mistakes feeling like the player's own fault. Example: Nergigante's thorns grow as it becomes aggressive. [Wikipedia, Monster Hunter: World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_Hunter:_World) (fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 158,077 characters; released 26 Jan 2018 on console, 9 Aug 2018 on Windows). Weak-point design is not described there.
- Fujioka (art director) and Tsujimoto (producer): realistic monsters are designed as part of a believable ecosystem and with their world in mind; early tutorial monsters have simple movesets with clear tells (a visible sign that a charge is coming) so players learn to dodge and counterattack; harder monsters come later. The Banbaro keeps a simple core attack, a direct charge, and its two sets of large antlers were added to support its charging concept. [VICE interview by Patrick Klepek](https://vice.com/en/article/the-developers-of-monster-hunter-explain-what-its-like-to-build-monsters) (18 Jun 2019; fetched 2026-10-28). The article does not discuss weak points.
- Capcom's own 2017 developer feature says early tests of the dense Ancient Forest were too cluttered to find monsters, so the team added Scout Flies, territory maps and lighting changes. [Capcom, Creative Minds](https://www.capcom.co.jp/ir/english/feature/2017_mh_crvoice.html) (added 17 Nov 2017; fetched 2026-10-28). It does not address animal logic, attack tells or weak points directly.
- Rise (Switch 26 Mar 2021, Windows 12 Jan 2022): monsters include Magnamalo (a tiger-like, mace-tailed wyvern), Ibushi (a serpentine wind Elder Dragon) and Narwa; the Wirebug lets hunters ride monsters. No design notes on telegraphing or weak points. [Wikipedia, Monster Hunter Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_Hunter_Rise) (fetched 2026-10-28).
- Search snippets (unread): Fujioka says concepts often start from living animals such as cats and dogs, reflected in how monsters move ([Cubed3](https://cubed3.com/news/25506/1/how-the-monster-hunter-team-create-monsters.html)).

## Not found
- Any source on weak points readable from afar, or on silhouette rules. Both of those parts of the task are unanswered.

## Recommendations for us (not sourced rules)
1. Start each enemy and boss from a behaviour the player must learn, then give it a shape that shows that behaviour (large horns for a charge, as with the Banbaro). Fits T133 to T137.
2. Tutorial enemies get one simple attack with a clear tell; harder variants come later (T150 scaling).
3. State change on damage: an enemy or boss changes behaviour at health thresholds, with a visible change to the model (like the Nergigante thorns); fits boss phases (T148).
4. Show weak spots with a glowing part or material change on the model (a lantern, a gear), tested at the far camera, since no source tells us how Monster Hunter does it.
5. Keep arena clutter low so enemies stay findable (the Ancient Forest lesson).
