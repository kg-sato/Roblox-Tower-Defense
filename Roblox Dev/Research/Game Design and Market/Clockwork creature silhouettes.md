# Clockwork and steampunk creature silhouettes (T012)

Question: what do real mechanical animals look like, and how do we build gear-and-brass creatures that still read as animals?

## Real references (sourced)
- Mechanical duck, Jacques de Vaucanson, 1738 to 1739: quacked, flapped wings, and seemed to eat. Silver Swan (maker John Joseph Merlin, with James Cox), musical elephant (Hubert Martinet, 1774) and Cox's Peacock Clock (1781) are listed as animal automata. [Wikipedia, Automaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automaton) (fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 118,750 characters read). The page gives no visual detail on plating or joints.
- Silver Swan (1773): life-sized silver swan among silver leaves and fish, glass rods for water; clockwork plus music box; the head turns side to side, it preens and bends to catch a fish. [Wikipedia, Silver Swan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Swan_(automaton)) (fetched 2026-10-28).
- Tipu's Tiger (about 1795): painted wooden shell about half an inch thick, brass organ pipes, ivory keys, crank-driven bellows, a hidden side flap showing an organ; the tiger's top half lifts off for the mechanics; about 71 cm high and 172 cm long. [Wikipedia, Tipu's Tiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipu%27s_Tiger) (fetched 2026-10-28).
- Steampunk motifs (brass, iron, exposed cogs, goggles, pocket watches): see [[Mystic Victorian creature reference board]] and [Wikipedia, Steampunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steampunk) (fetched 2026-10-28).

## What this tells us (inference from the facts above, not a source claim)
- Each historic piece keeps a clear animal outline (swan neck and body, tiger crouch) and puts the mechanism in one or two spots (a hidden flap, a base of rods). The animal shape comes first; gears are secondary.
- Movement sells it: the swan's head turn and fish-catch, the duck's wing flap. One signature motion per creature matches T092.
- Real plating is shell plus a few metal accents, which suits our triangle cap better than gear-covered bodies.

## Silhouette rules for our brass creatures (recommendations)
1. Draw the animal outline first (hound, owl, crow, cat) and test in greyscale at 64 px (T087).
2. Limit exposed gears to 2 or 3 big readable ones (a chest dial, a shoulder cog, a tail spring); small cogs vanish at board distance.
3. Brass plates as large flat shapes with one seam line; avoid fine rivets.
4. Add one lift-off or hinge feature (an open chest panel, as on Tipu's Tiger) as the lore detail.
5. One moving part reads best (head turn, wing flap).
