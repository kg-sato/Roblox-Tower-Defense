# Art Nouveau ornament references (T047)

Question: what are the defining ornament features of Art Nouveau (plant lines, whiplash curves), and how can they give our UI frames and stage sets a consistent look?

## Facts (sourced)
**Style** ([Wikipedia, Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau), fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 426,499 characters read)
- Whiplash curves: sinuous, flowing lines that give a sense of movement. Plant and flower motifs with floral and vegetal arabesques. Deliberately asymmetric, off-balance compositions with opposing lines. Nature as the design model. Materials: wrought iron, glass, ceramics, later concrete.
- Key figures: Alphonse Mucha (posters, such as the 1895 poster for Sarah Bernhardt), Victor Horta (Hôtel Tassel), Hector Guimard (iron-and-glass Paris Métro entrances), Louis Comfort Tiffany (glass and lamps, 1900 Paris Exposition), Émile Gallé (sculpted glass).
- Dates: infobox c. 1883 to 1914; popular about 1890 to 1910 in the Belle Époque; peak at the 1900 Paris Exposition. The page gives no palette.

**Mucha poster layout** ([Wikipedia, Alphonse Mucha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonse_Mucha), fetched 2026-10-28)
- Gismonda (commissioned late December 1894, displayed 1 January 1895) made him famous. His theatre posters use a halo-like ornate arch behind the figure's head; hair curling into arabesque forms that fill the frame; delicate pastel colours and fine line work; sparse backgrounds (in Gismonda only Byzantine mosaic tiles behind the head); an ornate top area balancing a bottom with minimal text.

**Tiffany glass** ([Wikipedia, Tiffany glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_glass), fetched 2026-10-28)
- Favrile glass patented 1892; colours mixed in hot glass for an iridescent surface (compared to butterfly wings and peacock feathers); streamer, fracture, ripple, drapery, opalescent and ring-mottle techniques; motifs include twigs, grass, leaf veins, water, magnolia petals and water lilies (lamp, c. 1904 to 1915). Corona factory 1901 to 1932; the studio went bankrupt in 1932. The page does not mention copper foil, dragonflies or wisteria.

## How to use it (recommendations, not sourced)
1. **UI frames:** use one whiplash curve as a corner flourish and one plant line along edges; keep it asymmetric but balanced; limit to 3 to 4 curve types so the HUD stays clean at phone size (T083 tokens, T060 title).
2. **Poster-style cards and title:** Mucha's pattern (ornate arch behind the figure, richly decorated top, minimal text at the bottom) fits familiar cards and the reveal screen (T096, T057).
3. **Stained glass in the Cathedral set:** favour opalescent and iridescent colour mixes with plant motifs; fracture and streamer ideas translate to low-poly decals (T105 materials, T048 note).
4. **Palette:** the sources give no palette beyond Mucha's pastels and Tiffany's iridescence; choose our own and test for colour-blind safety.
5. **Mix with Victorian Gothic carefully:** Art Nouveau is a later movement (about 1890 to 1910) than the earlier Victorian Gothic of [[Victorian interiors and lamplight palettes]], so use it for the late sets and the cosmic turn, not Lampwick Lane's early look.
6. Keep line weight consistent between UI frames and in-world props.
