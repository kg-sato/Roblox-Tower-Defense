# Brass, verdigris and tarnish material references (T046)

Question: how do brass, verdigris and tarnish really look and age, so our clockwork and brass assets read as believable materials?

**Deviation from the task:** the task asked for Pinterest boards. Pinterest returned nothing readable and a real board needs the user's account, so this is a text reference from three Wikipedia pages. Search terms for a real board are at the bottom.

## Facts (sourced)
**Brass** (copper-zinc alloy; typically about 2:1 copper to zinc). Colour depends on zinc: red brasses (under about 15% zinc) look reddish (gilding metal, 95% Cu, 5% Zn, is nearly red); alpha brasses (over 65% Cu) are golden; alpha-beta (55 to 65% Cu) are brighter; beta (50 to 55% Cu) are among the brightest and least golden; white brasses (over 50% Zn) look silvery. Brass tarnishes with moisture, chlorides, acetates, ammonia and some acids. Copper reacts with sulfur to form a brown, then black, copper sulfide layer; in slightly acidic conditions such as urban rain this can oxidise to a green-blue copper carbonate patina, which may or may not protect the metal. Dezincification (zinc leaching in seawater) leaves porous copper. [Wikipedia, Brass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass) (fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 136,219 characters).

**Verdigris:** a group of copper salts, from green to bluish-green depending on composition; a natural patina on copper, bronze and brass that can act as a protective layer. The article shows it as #43B3AE (RGB 67, 179, 174), ISCC-NBS "brilliant bluish green"; it can darken or brown with age. In marine settings the main salt is tribasic copper chloride. [Wikipedia, Verdigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verdigris) (fetched 2026-10-28).

**Patina:** a thin surface layer from oxidation. On copper alloys natural patina takes many years, faster in damp coastal air. Clean rural air gives basic copper carbonate (familiar green); urban or industrial air gives mainly copper sulfide or sulfate compounds. For applied patinas, chloride tends to give green and sulfur compounds (liver of sulfur) tend to give brown; ammonium sulfide gives blue-black, cupric nitrate blue-green, ferric nitrate yellow-brown. The article does not state a brown-then-green sequence as a rule. [Wikipedia, Patina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patina) (fetched 2026-10-28).

## How this maps to our assets (recommendations, not sourced)
1. **Environment decides the wear:** Harbour of Brass (damp, coastal) gets the most green-blue verdigris; Clockwork Foundry (industrial air) gets brown-black sulfide darkening; Silent Cathedral (dry, indoor) stays closer to dull gold. This follows the patina source's environment rules and gives each stage set a different material look (T124, T126).
2. **Layer the material in three tiers:** clean brass (golden, bright), brown-black tarnish in recesses and joints, and verdigris on edges and exposed corners where moisture sits; verdigris base colour near #43B3AE as a starting value.
3. **Use brighter, less golden brass for highlights** (beta brass) and redder brass for old or low-grade parts to vary a family of props (T105 material library).
4. **Keep emissive rules separate:** brass reflects lamp colour; only lantern glass and Ward crystal glow (T128).
5. Build one recipe in the Roblox material tools (MaterialVariant, T105) and test at phone distance before making more.
