# Victorian interiors and lamplight palettes (T045)

## Interiors (sourced)
Source: [Wikipedia, Victorian decorative arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_decorative_arts) (fetched 2026-10-28). The Victorian era ran 1837 to 1901.
- Hallways were often dull grey so they did not compete with neighbouring rooms; walls and woodwork were marbled or grained; ceilings were tinted a few shades lighter than the walls; colour pairs came from neighbouring or opposing hues on the wheel.
- Early wallpaper used dense floral patterns on primary red, blue and yellow grounds overprinted with cream and tan; later Gothic-inspired papers used earthy tones with stylised leaves; William Morris (major designs about 1875 to 1885) drew on medieval and Gothic tapestries; embossed papers were used on ceilings and friezes. Lincrusta, an embossed oil-based covering that could mimic wood or leather, dates from 1863.
- A bare room was considered poor taste: surfaces were filled with objects. No single furniture style dominated; Gothic and Rococo Revival were the most common; the dining-room sideboard was the ornate focal point.
- The page does not mention deep reds or greens, or stained glass.

## Lamplight (sourced)
- Gas street lighting: Salford 1806 (first in the world per the article); a London gas company created by the 1810 act; Westminster Bridge lit on 31 Dec 1813; Baltimore's first gas street lamp on 7 Feb 1817; the Welsbach gas mantle in 1891 (the theatre section says 1885), giving about three times the light of a naked flame. Gas lit homes, factories, churches and theatres. The page does not describe the colour or flicker of the light. [Wikipedia, Gas lighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_lighting) (fetched 2026-10-28; first 100,000 of 112,812 characters).
- Colour temperature: candle flame about 1,850 K (like sunrise or sunset), standard incandescent about 2,400 K, soft white about 2,550 K, horizon daylight about 5,000 K, overcast about 6,500 K. In art, 2,700 to 3,000 K reads "warm" and above 5,000 K reads "cool" (the reverse of the physics). [Wikipedia, Color temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_temperature) (fetched 2026-10-28).

## Calculation I ran (approximation)
Using the common Tanner Helland kelvin-to-RGB approximation (from memory, so unverified), I computed: 1850 K = #FF8100; 2400 K = #FF9B3D; 2700 K = #FFA757; 5000 K = #FFE4CE; 6500 K = #FFFEFA. These are screen approximations of a black body, not a measured lamp, and should be tuned by eye in Studio (Lighting.Ambient, PointLight.Color). Treat as starting values only.

## Recommendations (not sourced)
1. Lamp colour for Lampwick Lane: start from #FF9B3D (about 2400 K) and warm a candle prop to #FF8100; use a cool grey-blue ambient for fog (about 6500 K) so the lamps pop by contrast.
2. Interiors: dull grey halls, richer red/green/gold rooms, ceilings a few shades lighter than walls, and cluttered surfaces (stage sets 3 and 4, props T126).
3. Keep one ornate focal object per set (the sideboard idea): an altar, a clock, a furnace.
4. Check the final colours for contrast and colour-blind safety (T088, accessibility note).
