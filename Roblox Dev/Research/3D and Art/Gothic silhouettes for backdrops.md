# Gothic architecture silhouettes for backdrops (T049)

Question: which Gothic shapes read as a skyline from the board camera, and what rhythm should our Silent Cathedral and other backdrops use?

All pages fetched 2026-10-28. The sources describe the forms; silhouette and rhythm rules for a game backdrop are my recommendations.

## Facts (sourced)
**Elements** ([Wikipedia, Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture); first 100,000 of 367,212 characters)
- Pointed arches are the signature, used systematically; rib vaults; flying buttresses let walls be thinner and taller with larger windows; windows grew from early lancets to bar tracery and, in Rayonnant work, walls nearly all glass; pinnacles flank window arches in Flamboyant work; paired west towers with triple doors on the façade (Notre-Dame de Paris). Height was pursued: Chartres about 38 m; Beauvais about 48 m before a collapse (the page dates it 1248). Periods: Early c. 1140 to 1190; High c. 1190 to 1240; Rayonnant and Decorated c. 1240 to 1350; Late (Flamboyant and Perpendicular) c. 1350 to 1500. The spire section was cut off in the text I received.

**Flying buttress** ([Wikipedia, Flying buttress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_buttress))
- A sloping arch runs from the upper wall over the side-aisle roof to a heavy freestanding pier; designs often stack two arches (vault thrust below, wind load above). Piers are topped with a cone or pyramid, often with crockets, whose weight helps the pier resist the arch's push. The open arches and tall piers create see-through space and strong vertical lines. Early examples: Saint-Germain-des-Prés (1163), Saint-Remi (c. 1170), Notre-Dame de Paris (1180).

**Spire types** ([Wikipedia, Spire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spire))
- Needle spire (very tall and narrow, from a tower ringed by a parapet), broach spire (octagonal on a square tower with triangular transitions), crown spire (open arched structure like a crown, supported by buttresses), flèche (small roof spirelet). Historic heights: Lincoln Cathedral about 160 m (medieval, later collapsed), Cologne Cathedral 157 m (completed 1880), Rouen flèche 151 m, Salisbury 123 m (completed 1320).

## Silhouette and rhythm for a backdrop (recommendations, not sourced)
1. **Rhythm:** alternate tall and short vertical shapes along the skyline: spire, then a run of lancet-topped gables, then pinnacle-capped buttress piers, then a tower pair. A regular tick of piers with pinnacles gives the see-through comb effect the buttress page describes.
2. **Few large shapes:** at board-camera distance only the pointed-arch outline, the spire tip and the pier-and-arch comb survive; skip tracery detail and use a flat dark mass with a few lit windows (T162 landmarks).
3. **Three landmarks per set:** a spire (needle or broach), a paired-tower façade and a buttressed nave, placed at different distances for depth.
4. **Value structure:** dark silhouette against fog and a lighter sky so the shapes read in greyscale (the same test as familiars, T087).
5. **Use period consistently:** High Gothic lancets and spires for the Cathedral; leave Flamboyant pinnacle ornament and Art Nouveau curves for late sets.
6. **Budget:** build from a few repeated meshes (pier, lancet gable, spire) under the 20,000 triangle limit (see [[Mobile performance limits]]).
